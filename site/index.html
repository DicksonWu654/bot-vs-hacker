A three-part series on the short race around an Ethereum exploit: who saw it, who paid to go first, and where the money went

The bot beat the hacker.

When an exploit is in flight, some of the fastest software on Ethereum notices, copies it, pays the block builder to go first, and takes the money. Sometimes it gives the money back.

Read part 1: the raceWatch it here

On Ethereum, a transaction can wait in a public queue before it is written into a block. A block builder puts the block together and a proposer publishes it; a copying bot can pay the builder to run its copy first.

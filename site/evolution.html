      Words used on this page

      Public mempool
      The shared waiting room where pending transactions are visible to every node before they land in a block.
      Seen in the public mempool
      The Mempool Dumpster archive recorded the transaction before its block was proposed. Not seen, when the archive saw many of the other transactions in the same block, is strong evidence that the transaction went privately to a builder.
      Ordinary transactions in the same blocks
      The other transactions in each exploit's block. Their seen share is the control for how much the archive was catching that day.
      Constructor-only exploit
      The attack runs inside the attack contract's constructor, so creating the contract and attacking are one transaction.
      No watchable window
      A constructor-only exploit, an attack contract created inside the attack transaction, or one deployed in the same block as the attack.
      Fresh deploy-to-attack gap
      Time from the block that created an attacker-owned contract to the block of the attack, for contracts created at most seven days before.
      Paid the builder
      A direct ETH transfer to the block's fee recipient (the builder) inside the transaction. It is how a transaction buys its place in the block.
      Deploy-window copy
      A bot used the attacker's own on-chain setup (a deployed contract, an upgrade or a prepare() call) before the attacker's own attack call landed.
      Sample
      Ethereum mainnet exploits reproduced in the DeFiHackLabs repository, with the sender of each listed transaction classified. It is a sample of public, reproduced incidents, not of all attacks.

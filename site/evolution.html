What can be measured, and when

The public mempool archive we rely on begins on 7 August 2023. Before that, nobody saved which transactions were pending, so for older exploits we have in-block evidence only. Every number below says which part of the timeline it comes from.

Almost no exploit transaction shows up in the public archive

The comparison group matters. A transaction missing from the archive could be private or simply missed by it, so we count how many other transactions in the same blocks were in the archive. If the archive were poor, the blue line would be low too.

More attacks happen in a single transaction

A constructor-only exploit runs entirely while its contract is being created. There is nothing pending to copy except the whole deployment, so a bot has to rebuild the contract with its own addresses. Whiskers are 95% Wilson intervals. Years with fewer than ten exploits are drawn faint.

The window between deploy and attack is shrinking

Only exploits whose attack contract was deployed within the previous seven days are drawn. A zero gap means the same block or the same transaction, so a bot sees nothing before the attack lands.

Attackers stopped bidding in the gas auction

The priority fee is what the transaction pays on top of the base fee. It collapsed for everyone after the Merge, so the useful comparison is against the block's own median. Exploits with a zero tip cannot be drawn on a log scale; they are counted in the table.

Definitions

exposure: public or private
An exploit transaction is public if the mempool archive saw it before its block was proposed. It is private if the archive covered that day, saw many of the other transactions in the same block, and never saw this one. Otherwise the label is unknown. Private here means "not in the archive", which is strong evidence and not proof.
ordinary transactions in the same blocks
The share of other transactions in the exploit's block that the archive saw. This is the control for the archive's coverage on that day.
constructor-only exploit
The attack runs inside the attack contract's constructor, so deployment and attack are one transaction.
deploy-to-attack gap
Seconds between the block that created the attack contract and the block of the attack, only for contracts created within seven days before.
tip
Effective priority fee per gas, in gwei, of the first attack transaction. The block median is the median tip across all transactions in that block.
sample
Exploits listed in the DeFiHackLabs reproduction repository on Ethereum mainnet. It is a sample of public, reproduced incidents, not of all attacks.

DODO V2, 2021-03-08 - verdict: confirmed-onchain (two bots front-ran the real attacker; funds returned)

The real attacker (EOA 0x368a6558..., 'Mr. Hippo' in DODO's postmortem) abused the re-callable pool init(): stage fake tokens into a pool, then flashLoan and re-init the pool with the fake token pair so the repay check passes. At block 12000022 he staged one block earlier (12000020) and sent his attack at 132 gwei; bot 'Antelope' (EOA 0x35541875..., contract 0x00000000e84f...) landed a flashLoan + init() at index 4 / 243 gwei and emptied the pool (955,815 WSZO + 259,939 USDT), so the attacker's tx at index 176 reverted. The init() calldata is byte-identical to the attacker's, with his fake tokens; only the flashLoan receiver/data differ. Same pattern at block 12000102 (bot index 0 at 1883 gwei vs attacker 600 gwei). The attacker then deployed a bypass contract that stages and attacks in one tx (block 12000165, 1.14M USDT + 133.5k wCRES), but a third party, 'Cheetah' (EOA 0x8a14ce0f... via proxy 0xa57bd001...), called his contract's withdraw with byte-identical calldata at 684 gwei, beating both the attacker (142 gwei) and Antelope (652 gwei, out of gas). Antelope returned 955,815 WSZO, 101,548 ETHA, 1,000 wCRES, 296,009 USDT and 114.5 ETH to 0xdad3e839 on 2021-03-10. Cheetah's ~$1.89M return is reported, not found on-chain. The attacker kept ~$200k from smaller attacks.

In-block evidence only (pre-Merge, no mempool archive, no coinbase payments).

Three facts: (1) init() args byte-identical between bot and attacker, bot one block after the attacker's stage tx and ahead by index; (2) Cheetah's replay of the attacker's own withdraw calldata won a gas war against Antelope by 32 gwei; (3) the 93,148 gwei / 8.1 ETH Antelope tx in the folklore is a different race (pool 0x236116ff vs another EOA), not the attacker's.
